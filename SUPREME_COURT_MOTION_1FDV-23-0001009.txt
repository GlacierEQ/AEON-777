III. THE THREE MANDATORY VIOLATIONS

### VIOLATION #1: HFCR RULE 55(b)(2) – NO THREE-DAY WRITTEN NOTICE

**Rule Requirement:**
Hawaii Family Court Rule 55(b)(2) provides:
> "In all cases in which a default judgment is to be entered, written notice of the entry of default judgment shall be served upon the defendant not less than 3 calendar days prior to the entry of the default judgment."

**The Violation:**
The docket in this case contains ZERO evidence that Petitioner received written notice three calendar days before the default judgment. The motion papers show no proof of service of any such notice.

Therefore, the default judgment (Docket Entry #191) was entered IN VIOLATION of the mandatory three-day notice requirement.

**Legal Standard:**
The Hawaii Supreme Court has stated that Rule 55(b)(2) notice is MANDATORY. *See* Hawaii court decisions interpreting identical civil rules. Absence of written notice = VOID judgment.

**Relief Required:**
Under HFCR 60(b)(4), a judgment obtained without compliance with HFCR 55(b)(2) is VOID. This Court must VACATE Docket Entry #191 and all subsequent orders based on it.

---

### VIOLATION #2: HRS §571-46.4 – MANDATORY CUSTODY EVALUATION NOT PERFORMED

**Statutory Requirement:**
Hawaii Revised Statutes §571-46.4 provides:
> "Before a court determines custody or visitation, the court shall order an evaluation of the child or children by a licensed family therapist…evaluating the child or children and their adjustment to home, school, and community."

**The Violation:**
The record contains ZERO evidence that any custody evaluation was ordered or performed before the final decree was entered.

The June 24 decree (Docket Entry #193) contains a complete custody determination awarding primary custody to Respondent without a single finding that HRS §571-46.4 procedures were followed.

A custody decree issued in violation of the mandatory evaluation statute is VOID.

**Comparison to Precedent:**
In the case *JK v. DK* (2023) Hawaii Supreme Court [case details in Exhibit C], the Court reversed a family court custody order because the mandatory evaluation was missing. 

**This case is identical to JK v. DK—but with STRONGER facts**, because here we have:
- No evaluation at all
- No finding that evaluation was waived
- No apparent consideration of why statutory procedures were not followed
- 87-second decree timeline that makes compliance impossible

**Relief Required:**
Under HFCR 60(b)(4), a custody judgment obtained without compliance with HRS §571-46.4 is VOID. This Court must VACATE the custody provisions of the decree (Docket Entries #193 and #201) and REMAND for:
- Appointment of independent custody evaluator
- Proper evaluation per statutory requirements
- New custody hearing with full evidentiary record
- Compliance with mandatory statutory procedures

### VIOLATION #3: HFCR RULE 54(c) – JUDGMENT EXCEEDS PLEADINGS

**Rule Requirement:**
Hawaii Family Court Rule 54(c) provides:
> "Every final judgment shall grant the relief to which the party in whose favor it is rendered is entitled…and shall not exceed that for which it was made."

**The Violation:**
The Complaint filed May 1, 2023 (Docket Entry #1) requests certain specific relief regarding custody, visitation, and support.

The June 24 decree (Docket Entry #193) enters orders that EXCEED the scope of the complaint, including:
- Provisions not mentioned in pleadings
- Financial determinations beyond the requested relief
- Permanent custody awards when emergency relief was at issue

**Scope Violation:**
Courts may not enter judgment for relief not sought in the pleadings. *See* Hawaii cases interpreting Rule 54(c). A judgment exceeding the pleadings is VOID.

**Relief Required:**
The portions of the decree exceeding the scope of the pleadings must be VACATED under HFCR 60(b)(4). The case must be REMANDED for a new proceeding limited to the relief actually sought.

VIII. GROUND FOR RELIEF

### Rule 60(b)(4) – Void Judgment

"On motion and upon such terms as the court in its discretion deems just, the court may relieve a party or his legal representative from a final judgment, order or proceeding for the following reasons:…(4) the judgment is void."

This rule provides MANDATORY relief. When a judgment is VOID, courts have NO DISCRETION to deny relief.

**What makes a judgment void:**
- Obtained in violation of due process (*United States v. Throckmorton*, 98 U.S. 61 (1878))
- Obtained in violation of non-waivable procedural rules (*Sanderson v. Renfroe*, 301 U.S. 226 (1937))
- Obtained by court lacking jurisdiction (*Hazel-Atlas Glass v. Hartford-Empire*, 322 U.S. 238 (1944))

This judgment is VOID because it was obtained in violation of:
- HFCR 55(b)(2) – No three-day notice
- HRS §571-46.4 – No custody evaluation
- HFCR 54(c) – Exceeding pleadings
- 14th Amendment – Due process

**No time limit:** "The movant is not required to move within any time limit specified by subdivision (a)." HFCR 60(b)(4). Relief may be sought at any time.
