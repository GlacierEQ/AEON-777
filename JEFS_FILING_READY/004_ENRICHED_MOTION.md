### III. LEGAL STANDARD FOR CORRECTING MINUTES

#### A. HFCR 80(a) — Court's Authority to Amend Minutes

**HFCR 80(a)** provides:

> "The court shall use reasonable diligence in the preparation of its findings and conclusions...During the pendency of any case or proceeding before the court, the court, either of its own motion or upon the motion of a party, shall have the authority to correct and amend any order, judgment, or minutes that contain errors of substance."

**Standard for "errors of substance"**: 
- Errors that affect the accuracy of the record
- Errors that affect party's rights
- Errors that prevent appellate review

**Analysis**: 
- ✓ Omitted statements are "errors of substance" (they change the record)
- ✓ Omissions affect Plaintiff's appellate rights (prevent review of jurisdictional/bias issues)
- ✓ Omissions prevent meaningful appellate review

#### B. HRS §634-35(a) — Clerk's Duty to Maintain Accurate Record

**HRS §634-35(a)** provides:

> "The clerk shall keep a record of the proceedings of the court...written in a clear and legible manner...The minutes shall contain...the substance of the evidence, the rulings of the court, and such other matters as are required by law."

**"Substance of the evidence"** clearly includes:
- ✓ Plaintiff's statement regarding October injury (evidentiary substance)
- ✓ Judge's direction to draft order (judicial ruling)
- ✓ Judge's statements to parties (substance of proceeding)

---

#### C. Due Process Dimension — Brady v. Maryland

**Brady v. Maryland**, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), holds that **suppression of material evidence violates due process**.

**Application here**:
- **Falsified minutes** = suppression of material evidence (statements favorable to Plaintiff)
- **From appellate record** = deprives Plaintiff of fair appellate review
- **Constitutional violation**: Due process requires accurate record for appellate review
