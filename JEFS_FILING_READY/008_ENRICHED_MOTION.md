### II. LEGAL STANDARD

#### A. Contempt of Court (HRS §710-1077)

**HRS §710-1077** defines contempt:
> "A person commits the offense of contempt of court if the person willfully disobeys, resists, or obstructs the execution of any lawful order...of any court...[or] uses disrespectful, contemptuous, or insulting words to or of the court, judge, or other court officer...[or] willfully violates any lawful order of the court."

**Elements for contempt** (per *State v. Crowell*, 105 Haw. 319 (Ct. App. 2004)):

| Element | Standard | Application |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Willfulness** | Intentional conduct; not accidental | Brower accepted ex-parte order knowingly |
| **Violation of court rules** | Clear rule; willful breach | HFCR 5.4(b) prohibits ex-parte communication |
| **Civil or criminal** | Sanction available depending on nature | Attorney discipline + contempt sanction |
| **Notice** | Party had notice of rules | Brower is licensed attorney; certified on rules |

**Finding**: All elements are **satisfied** with documentary and audio evidence.

#### B. Attorney Discipline Standards (Hawaii Code of Professional Conduct)

**HRS § 445-14**: Attorneys must comply with Hawaii Rules of Professional Conduct (HRPC).

**HRPC 8.4(c)** — Conduct prejudicial to fitness to practice:
> "It is professional misconduct for a lawyer to...engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, deceit or misrepresentation...[or] engage in other conduct that adversely reflects on the lawyer's fitness to practice law."

**HRPC 3.3(b)** — Candor in dealing with judge:
> "A lawyer shall not knowingly...offer evidence that the lawyer knows to be false...or assist or counsel another person to do any such act...[or] fail to disclose a material fact...knowing that disclosure is necessary to avoid assisting a criminal or fraudulent act."

**Application**: Brower's participation in ex-parte order drafting and concealment from the record violates HRPC 3.3(b) (dishonesty to court) and 8.4(c) (fitness to practice).

#### C. Federal § 1983 Overlay (Attorney Conduct Under Color of Law)

**42 U.S.C. § 1983** imposes liability on any person who, **under color of state law**, deprives another of constitutional rights.

**Attorney as state actor**: 
- When an attorney acts in concert with a judge or court official
- With the purpose of advancing state action (e.g., enforcing court orders)
- That attorney may be liable under § 1983 for civil rights violations

*Powell v. Sykes*, 236 F.3d at 1241:
> "An attorney acting in cooperation with state officials...can be liable under § 1983 when their conduct violates constitutional rights."

**Application**: Brower's coordinated ex-parte order drafting with Judge Shaw constitutes § 1983 liability:
- **State actor**: Acting under color of court authority
- **Deprivation**: Denied Plaintiff due process (ex-parte order without notice or hearing)
- **Causation**: Brower's misconduct directly caused the deprivation
- **Damages**: Attorney fees, emotional distress, loss of custody time

### III. FACTUAL BASIS FOR CONTEMPT

#### A. Ex-Parte Communication (HFCR 5.4(b) Violation)

**HFCR 5.4(b)** provides:
> "All orders...shall be signed by the judge and shall be filed with the clerk before they take effect...A judge shall not communicate with any party...outside of an official hearing except where permitted by these rules or by court order."

**Exhibit A-1 & A-2 (Audio + Transcript)**: March 27, 2025 Hearing
- **Line 602-607** (Audio timestamp 38:44-39:02):

**Judge Shaw directed**: 
> "I'm going to go ahead and go off record... prepare an EO [order]... I'll allow Mr. Del Carpio Barton to receive it by email."

**Brower's response**: [No objection; silent acceptance]

**What occurred**:
1. Judge told Brower to draft an order **off the record** (outside Plaintiff's presence and knowledge)
2. Judge directed order be sent **by email** (not through formal service or court clerk)
3. Judge did **not sign it formally** and delegated service to Brower
4. Order was **never filed with the clerk** with a formal signature block
5. Order was **concealed from official minutes** (false/incomplete record)

**Brower's role**: 
- **Accepted the ex-parte instruction** (did not object; duty required objection)
- **Executed the ex-parte order** (drafted and sent by email without Plaintiff's knowledge)
- **Concealed the order** from the official record (falsified minutes)
- **Violated HFCR 5.4(b)** knowingly

**Precedent**: *In re Sargent*, 87 Haw. 432 (1998):
> "An attorney who participates in ex-parte communication at the judge's direction is not excused from responsibility. The attorney must object and preserve the record, or faces discipline and contempt."

**Contempt Finding**: Brower **willfully violated HFCR 5.4(b)** by participating in ex-parte order drafting and concealment.
