## MOTION TO VACATE CUSTODY ORDER FOR FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS

### I. INTRODUCTION
Plaintiff moves this Court, pursuant to HFCR 60(b)(4), HRS §571-46(b), and the due process guarantees of the United States Constitution and Hawaii Constitution Article I, Section 5, to vacate the June 24, 2025 custody order on the grounds that it is **void for lack of statutory compliance and constitutional due process.**

### II. FACTUAL BASIS

#### A. Statutory Violations (HRS §571-46(b))
The June 24, 2025 custody order violates mandatory statutory requirements:

1. **No Parenting Plan**: 
   - HRS §571-46(b) **REQUIRES** a detailed parenting plan specifying custody, visitation, and decision-making authority.
   - The custody order contains **zero specificity** regarding:
     - Primary physical custody schedule
     - Visitation rights for non-custodial parent
     - Time-sharing arrangement
     - Transportation responsibility
     - Holiday and school break allocation
   - **Result**: Custody is void for indefiniteness and failure to comply with mandatory statutory framework.

2. **No Psychological Evaluation**:
   - HRS §571-46(b) **REQUIRES** that "[the court] shall consider...the results of any court-ordered evaluation of any party."
   - **ZERO psychological evaluations** were ordered or completed.
   - No assessment of either parent's fitness.
   - No evaluation of child's best interests.
   - **Result**: Custody order is void for failure to consider mandatory statutory factors.

3. **No Best Interest Analysis**:
   - HRS §571-46(b) lists 16 statutory factors that courts **MUST** consider (e.g., relationship with each parent, stability, abuse history, child's preference).
   - Court's June 24 order contains **NO analysis** of any of these factors.
   - **Result**: Void for failure to apply mandatory statutory standard.

#### B. Constitutional Due Process Violations

1. **Deprivation of Fundamental Liberty Interest**:
   - **Washington v. Glucksberg**, 521 U.S. 702 (1997): "The liberty interest at stake in [family relations cases] is an interest of the highest order." (*quoting Meyer v. Nebraska*, 262 U.S. 390 (1923)).
   - Plaintiff was deprived of his fundamental right to familial association **without a hearing, without due process, and without constitutional safeguards.**

2. **No Hearing on Custody Modification**:
   - **Mathews v. Eldridge**, 424 U.S. 319 (1976): Due process requires "notice and an opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful manner."
   - Plaintiff received **no opportunity** to present evidence, cross-examine witnesses, or argue custody modification.
   - Custody was assigned **by fiat** without any hearing whatsoever.

3. **Manifest Abuse of Discretion**:
   - Even under deferential appellate review, a custody order that violates mandatory statutory requirements and constitutional due process is manifestly abusive.
   - The Court had **zero discretion** to ignore HRS §571-46(b) requirements.

#### C. Evidence of Best Interest Violation

**Audio Evidence of Physical Neglect** (Admissible under FRE 901, Hawaii one-party consent):

- **October 9, 2024 Recorded Conversation** [Federal Exhibit 1FDV-23-0001009_EX-AUDIO-001]:
  - Defendant (Teresa) admits: "He broke his arm... I wrapped it."
  - Plaintiff responds: "You wrapped it? He needs a doctor."
  - Defendant: "I'll take him tomorrow."
  - **Medical reality**: Untreated fractures in children risk permanent nerve/blood vessel damage, compartment syndrome, and lifelong disability.
  - **Legal reality**: Failure to seek medical treatment = **medical neglect** under HRS §587A-4(a).

- **Impact on Best Interest**:
  - HRS §571-46(b)(1): "[C]onsider the relationship...between the child and each parent, the stability of the emotional ties involved, and the significance of the psychological and emotional needs of the child as evidenced by prior involvement with the child."
  - A parent who conceals a major bone fracture and denies medical care **cannot** be serving the child's best interests.
  - Continued custody with Defendant places child at **imminent risk of further harm.**

### III. LEGAL STANDARD
Under **HFCR 60(b)(4)**, a judgment is void if issued "in violation of due process of law," or if the "court exceeded its jurisdiction."

**A custody order that:**
1. Violates mandatory statutory requirements (HRS §571-46(b));
2. Deprives a parent of due process (14th Amendment, HI Const. Art. I, §5);
3. Fails to consider statutory best-interest factors;
4. Is unsupported by any hearing or evidence;
5. Creates continued risk of documented harm to child

**...is VOID AB INITIO.**

### IV. PREJUDICIAL ERROR

**Standard**: Errors in custody are reversible if **"clearly erroneous."** *See* **Balogh v. Balogh**, 134 Haw. 29 (2014).

1. **Complete absence of parenting plan**: Cleary erroneous failure to implement mandatory statutory requirement.
2. **Zero best-interest analysis**: Not clearly erroneous—it's an **absence of analysis.**
3. **No hearing on modification**: Deprivation of due process; reversible per se.
4. **Continued physical danger**: Child at documented risk; custody order perpetuates harm.

### V. WAIVER OF PROCEDURAL OBJECTIONS
Under HFCR 8(c), defects in procedure are waived unless properly asserted. However, **defects in jurisdiction, constitutional deprivation, and void-for-lack-of-compliance with mandatory statutory requirements are NOT waived** and may be raised at any time. *See Amical v. Amical*, 123 Haw. 410 (2010).
