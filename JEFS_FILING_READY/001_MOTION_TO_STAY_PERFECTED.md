## MOTION TO STAY PROCEEDINGS PENDING APPEAL

### I. INTRODUCTION
Plaintiff moves this Court, pursuant to HFCR 62(a) and HFCR 73, for an immediate stay of all proceedings in the above-captioned action pending resolution of Plaintiff's appeal to the Hawaii Supreme Court. The underlying judgment and decree are subject to substantial constitutional challenges that warrant a stay pending appellate resolution.

### II. FACTUAL BASIS

#### A. Procedural Violations Without Cure
1. **March 27, 2025 Hearing Transcript (Lines 321-328, 40-56, 80-84, 602-607)**:
   - Plaintiff's jurisdiction objection ("He broke his arm in October, and nobody put it on the docket") was stated on record but NOT included in the minutes filed with the Court.
   - Judge Shaw directed Brower to draft an order "off-record" and email it without Plaintiff's signature, violating HFCR 5.4(b) and HRS §634-35(a).
   - Plaintiff was belittled by the Court ("You're not listening") without legitimate judicial purpose.

2. **April 2025 Settlement Conference (Default Judgment)**:
   - Plaintiff was converted to default despite being only 8 minutes late to a settlement conference.
   - HFCR 55(b)(2) requires **notice and opportunity to appear** before entry of default.
   - No notice was provided that the conference would result in default.
   - An 8-minute delay does not constitute willful violation of a court order.

3. **Falsified Minutes (June 2025)**:
   - The official docket minutes omit material statements from the March 27 hearing.
   - Plaintiff has audio recording (42 minutes, 660-line transcript) authenticating the discrepancy.
   - Record falsification constitutes fraud on the court under HFCR 60(b)(3).

#### B. Constitutional Deprivation
1. **Void Ab Initio Custody Order**:
   - No parenting plan was entered; custody assigned by fiat (Tr. 658-660).
   - No psychological evaluation (HRS §571-46(b) requirement).
   - Child removed from Plaintiff without constitutional due process.

2. **Denial of Due Process**:
   - 380-day delay on Motion for Pre-Decree Relief filed May 16, 2024.
   - No hearing; no ruling; no explanation.
   - Constitutes deprivation under the Fourteenth Amendment and Hawaii Constitution Article I, Section 5.

### III. LEGAL STANDARD
Under HFCR 62(a), a stay pending appeal may be granted where:
1. Plaintiff has raised substantial legal questions;
2. Plaintiff will likely suffer irreparable harm absent a stay;
3. The balance of hardships favors a stay.

**All three factors are satisfied here.**

### IV. SUBSTANTIAL LEGAL QUESTIONS
1. **Constitutional**: Did the state court exceed jurisdiction and deny due process by (a) ignoring statutory requirements, (b) falsifying records, and (c) severing parental rights without hearing?
2. **Procedural**: Can a trial court direct an attorney to draft orders ex-parte without defendant's signature?
3. **Jurisdictional**: Can default judgment be entered without proper notice and opportunity to appear?

### V. IRREPARABLE HARM
**Absence of stay = irreparable harm to Plaintiff and minor child:**
- Loss of parent-child relationship (cannot be restored once severed)
- Documented physical neglect (October 2024 broken arm concealment) will continue unchecked
- Plaintiff's fundamental liberty interest in familial association is being extinguished

### VI. BALANCE OF HARDSHIPS
- **Hardship to Plaintiff/Child**: Constitutional rights destroyed; irreplaceable parent-child time lost daily
- **Hardship to Defendant**: Mere delay of an unlawfully-obtained default decree
- **Public Interest**: Judicial integrity; correction of record falsification; protection of constitutional rights

### VII. GOOD FAITH CERTIFICATION
Plaintiff certifies that this motion is filed in good faith and not for delay. The substantial legal questions and evidence of procedural violation support emergency relief.
