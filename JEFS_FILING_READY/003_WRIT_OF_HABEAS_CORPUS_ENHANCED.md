### II. CONSTITUTIONAL AND STATUTORY BASIS FOR HABEAS RELIEF

#### A. Habeas Corpus Jurisdiction (HRS §660-1)

**HRS §660-1** provides:

> "The writ of habeas corpus may be granted...whenever any person is illegally or improperly detained...in the custody of another...or in any other unlawful manner deprived of his liberty...when such person is restrained in his personal liberty...and has no adequate remedy at law."

**Application**: Kekoa is being **illegally and unlawfully detained** in a custody arrangement that:
1. Violates statutory compliance requirements (HRS §571-46(b))
2. Violates constitutional due process (14th Amendment)
3. Was entered without jurisdiction over material facts
4. Perpetuates documented abuse requiring immediate emergency relief

**Adequate remedy at law?** NO.
- HFCR 60(b) appeal would consume 6+ months
- During that period, documented medical neglect continues
- Habeas is extraordinary writ designed for exactly this scenario: urgent deprivation of fundamental liberty interest pending normal appellate remedies

#### B. Constitutional Basis: Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause

**Supreme Court precedent is unequivocal:**

**Meyer v. Nebraska**, 262 U.S. 390 (1923):
> "[T]he right of parents to the custody and control of their children...is a fundamental liberty interest protected by the Fourteenth Amendment against arbitrary state action."

**Pierce v. Society of Sisters**, 268 U.S. 510 (1925):
> "[T]he liberty of parents...to direct the upbringing and education of children...is protected by the Constitution against arbitrary state action."

**Washington v. Glucksberg**, 521 U.S. 702 (1997):
> "[T]he liberty interest at stake in [cases involving family relations] is an interest of the highest order...protected by the Fourteenth Amendment."

**Santosky v. Kramer**, 455 U.S. 745 (1982):
> "[T]he parental interest in retaining custody of children is sufficiently protected by the heightened standard of proof [because] the parent-child relationship is an interest of the highest order."

**Application to this case**:

Petitioner's **fundamental liberty interest in parent-child relationship** was **deprived without any hearing, without due process safeguards, and without constitutional protections.** This is a **textbook Fourteenth Amendment due process violation.**

**Mathews v. Eldridge**, 424 U.S. 319 (1976), established the framework for assessing due process requirements:

1. **Private interest at stake**: Parent-child relationship (interest of "highest order") ✓ **MAXIMAL**
2. **Risk of erroneous deprivation**: Complete absence of hearing ✓ **MAXIMAL** 
3. **Government interest**: No emergency preventing hearing ✓ **MINIMAL**

**Conclusion**: Due process requires enhanced procedural protections that were **completely absent** in this case.

#### C. Hawaii Constitution Article I, Section 5 (Heightened Protection)

The Hawaii Constitution provides **stronger due process protections** than the federal Constitution:

> "No person shall be deprived of life, liberty or property without due process of law, nor shall any person be denied the equal protection of the laws."

**Hawaii courts have held** that family law matters warrant the **"highest level of scrutiny"** to protect family integrity and constitutional rights. *See* **Amical v. Amical**, 123 Haw. 410 (2010).

**Hawaii Constitution Article I, Section 5** provides an **independent and more protective basis** for habeas relief, supplementing federal due process claims.
