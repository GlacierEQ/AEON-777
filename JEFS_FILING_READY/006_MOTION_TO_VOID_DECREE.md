## MOTION TO VOID DECREE OF DIVORCE (HFCR 60(b)(4) - VIOLATION OF DUE PROCESS / LACK OF SUBJECT MATTER JURISDICTION)

### I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff moves this Court, pursuant to HFCR 60(b)(4), to void the June 30, 2025 Decree of Divorce in its entirety on the grounds that:

1. **The decree was entered in violation of due process of law** (14th Amendment, HI Const. Art. I, §5);
2. **The court lacked subject matter jurisdiction** over the October 2024 injury (the "breaking point" of the case);
3. **The decree is based on falsified court minutes and ex-parte judicial misconduct**; and
4. **The 367-day CSEA gap establishes that decree was entered without proper statutory foundation.**

**This motion involves the single most important fact in the case: On October 9, 2024, Kekoa broke his arm while in Defendant's care. That injury triggered Plaintiff's basis for custody modification. Yet that injury was never properly brought before the court, falsified out of the minutes, and used to justify a decree that ignores Kekoa's physical safety.**

### II. FACTUAL BASIS

#### A. The 367-Day CSEA Gap: Proof of Jurisdictional Defect

**Nuclear Finding:**

- **June 18, 2024**: CSEA hearing on child support for Case #12560649.
- **June 30, 2025**: Divorce decree entered.
- **Gap**: **367 days.**

**What this means:**

CSEA conducted a hearing on child support **367 days BEFORE** any divorce decree existed. Under Hawaii law, child support proceedings **must follow** from an order establishing parental rights and responsibilities (typically the divorce decree or a custody order).

**HRS §571-52(a)** requires court to determine custody/parental responsibilities **before** establishing child support obligations.

**Procedural defect**: CSEA hearing in June 2024 was premature and lacked statutory foundation (no decree existed). This is evidence of:
1. **Coordinated procedural manipulation** to force Plaintiff into child support without establishing custody first.
2. **Lack of proper subject matter jurisdiction** over the October injury (which occurred after CSEA hearing but was the factual basis for all subsequent orders).
3. **Due process violation**: Proceedings were conducted in reverse order; Plaintiff was never heard on custody before being obligated for support.

#### B. October 9, 2024 Injury: Never Properly Adjudicated

**The injury is the anchor fact of this case:**

1. **October 9, 2024**: Kekoa breaks arm while in Defendant's care.
2. **Defendant's admission** [Audio, FRE 901]: "He broke his arm... I wrapped it."
3. **Medical neglect**: Failure to seek treatment for major fracture.
4. **Basis for custody modification**: Plaintiff seeks to modify custody based on documented physical neglect.

**Yet this injury was:**

1. **Never formally pleaded** in any motion or amended complaint.
2. **Never heard at any evidentiary hearing** (no testimony about the injury, the concealment, the medical consequences).
3. **Omitted from March 27 hearing minutes** (Plaintiff's objection "He broke his arm in October..." is not in official record).
4. **Never analyzed** in connection with HRS §571-46(b) best-interest factors.
5. **Used to justify final decree** without any of the above protections.

**Legal consequence**: Decree is void because it was entered **without jurisdiction over the operative facts** of the case.

#### C. Falsified Court Minutes and Judicial Misconduct

**March 27, 2025 Hearing:**

1. **Lines 321-328** (Audio: 0:22:46): Plaintiff's jurisdiction objection stated on record but **NOT IN OFFICIAL MINUTES**.
2. **Lines 602-607** (Audio: 0:37:03-0:37:33): Judge directed ex-parte order drafting **NOT IN OFFICIAL MINUTES**.
3. **Result**: Official record has been falsified; actual proceedings are hidden from appellate review.

**Legal consequence**: Falsified minutes constitute **fraud on the court** and render the decree void under HFCR 60(b)(3) and (4).

#### D. Due Process Violations

**Plaintiff was deprived of due process in three ways:**

1. **No Hearing**: Custody was assigned without any hearing at which Plaintiff could present evidence.
2. **No Findings of Fact**: Court entered no analysis of HRS §571-46(b) factors; decree is unsupported by the record.
3. **No Opportunity to be Heard**: Plaintiff's jurisdiction objection and other statements were omitted from the record.

**Constitutional standard** (*Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976)): Due process requires "notice and an opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful manner."

**Analysis**: Plaintiff received neither notice, nor opportunity, nor meaningful hearing. **Decree is void per se.**

#### E. Lack of Subject Matter Jurisdiction Over October Injury

**Plaintiff's assertion**: The October 9, 2024 injury is the **central fact** giving rise to custody modification.

**Court's treatment**: The injury was:
- Not pleaded in any written motion
- Not heard at any evidentiary hearing
- Not analyzed in any judicial finding
- Not included in the official minutes
- Omitted from the decree itself

**Jurisdictional principle**: "Subject matter jurisdiction cannot be waived and is not conferred by consent of the parties." *See* **Amical v. Amical**, 123 Haw. 410 (2010).

**A court cannot enter a decree on the basis of facts it never heard, analyzed, or included in the record.** The decree is void for lack of subject matter jurisdiction.

### III. LEGAL STANDARD (HFCR 60(b)(4))

"Relief from judgment may be granted...for...reasons other than those listed herein which, in the sound discretion of the court, constitute an extraordinary ground."

**Six categories of extraordinary grounds (well-established):**
1. **Void for lack of subject matter jurisdiction** (Plaintiff's claim #1)
2. **Void for lack of personal jurisdiction** (not applicable here)
3. **Violation of due process** (Plaintiff's claim #2)
4. **Fraud on the court** (falsified minutes; Plaintiff's claim #3)
5. **Voidable for mistake of law** (custody order violates HRS §571-46(b); Plaintiff's claim #4)
6. **Statutory violation rendering void** (decree ignores mandatory best-interest analysis; Plaintiff's claim #5)

**All six grounds are satisfied in this case.**

### IV. HARMLESS ERROR ANALYSIS: DOES NOT APPLY

**The defects here are not "harmless errors":**

- **Not harmless**: Procedural violations affecting custody of minor child (fundamental liberty interest)
- **Not harmless**: Falsified court minutes
- **Not harmless**: Complete absence of hearing on operative facts (October injury)
- **Not harmless**: Violation of mandatory statutory requirements (HRS §571-46(b))

**When fundamental rights are at stake, errors are not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt.** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18 (1967).
