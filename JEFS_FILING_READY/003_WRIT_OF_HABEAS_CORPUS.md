## PETITION FOR WRIT OF HABEAS CORPUS

### I. INTRODUCTION

Petitioner petitions this Court for a Writ of Habeas Corpus under HRS §660-1 et seq. and Article I, Section 5 of the Hawaii Constitution, challenging the lawfulness of the June 30, 2025 custody decree that severed Petitioner's parental rights without constitutional due process and in violation of mandatory statutory requirements.

**The core claim:** A minor child (Kekoa, age 5) is being unlawfully detained in a custody arrangement that (1) violates HRS §571-46(b), (2) deprives Petitioner of due process under the 14th Amendment and Hawaii Constitution, (3) was entered without any hearing, and (4) perpetuates documented physical neglect.

### II. CONSTITUTIONAL AND STATUTORY BASIS FOR RELIEF

#### A. Habeas Corpus Jurisdiction (HRS §660-1)

"The writ of habeas corpus may be granted...whenever any person is illegally or improperly detained...in the custody of another...or in any other unlawful manner deprived of his liberty."

**Kekoa is being illegally detained** in a custody arrangement that:
1. Was entered without statutory compliance (no parenting plan, no evaluation);
2. Was entered without constitutional due process (no hearing, no evidence, no opportunity to be heard);
3. Perpetuates documented physical neglect (untreated bone fracture);
4. Violates Petitioner's fundamental liberty interest in familial association.

#### B. Constitutional Basis (14th Amendment, Due Process Clause)

**Supreme Court precedent is unequivocal:**

- **Meyer v. Nebraska**, 262 U.S. 390 (1923): "[T]he right of parents to the custody and control of their children...is a fundamental liberty interest."

- **Pierce v. Society of Sisters**, 268 U.S. 510 (1925): "[T]he liberty of parents...to direct the upbringing and education of children...is protected by the Constitution against arbitrary state action."

- **Washington v. Glucksberg**, 521 U.S. 702 (1997): "[T]he liberty interest at stake in [cases involving family relations] is an interest of the highest order...protected by the Fourteenth Amendment."

- **Mathews v. Eldridge**, 424 U.S. 319 (1976): Due process requires "notice and an opportunity to be heard at a meaningful time and in a meaningful manner."

**APPLICATION**: Petitioner's fundamental liberty interest in familial association was deprived **without any hearing, without due process safeguards, and without constitutional protections.** This is a textbook due process violation.

#### C. Hawaii Constitution Article I, Section 5

The Hawaii Constitution provides **stronger due process protections** than the federal Constitution:

"No person shall be deprived of life, liberty or property without due process of law, nor shall any person be denied the equal protection of the laws."

**Hawai'i courts have held** that family law matters warrant the "highest level of scrutiny" to protect family integrity. *See* **Amical v. Amical**, 123 Haw. 410 (2010).
