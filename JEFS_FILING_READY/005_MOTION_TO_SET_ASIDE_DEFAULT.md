## MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT JUDGMENT (SETTLEMENT CONFERENCE CONVERTED TO DEFAULT)

### I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff moves this Court, pursuant to HFCR 55(c) and HFCR 60(b), to set aside the default judgment entered on April 2, 2025, when Plaintiff was 8 minutes late to a settlement conference that Plaintiff did not receive proper notice was at risk of resulting in default.

**The entry of default was unlawful** because:
1. **No HFCR 55(b)(2) notice** was provided that default would result from non-appearance;
2. **No opportunity to appear** was afforded before default judgment was entered;
3. **Excusable neglect** (8-minute delay) does not justify default in family law matter involving custody of minor child;
4. **Meritorious defense** (multiple grounds to vacate underlying orders) would be prejudiced by default.

### II. FACTUAL BASIS

#### A. The April 2, 2025 Settlement Conference and Unlawful Default

1. **Notice Deficiency**:
   - Plaintiff was notified of settlement conference set for "April 2, 2025 at 1:30 PM."
   - **Plaintiff received NO notice that failure to appear would result in default judgment.**
   - **Plaintiff received NO notice that settlement conference could be converted to default proceeding.**
   - Under HFCR 55(b)(2), **entry of default requires affirmative notice** that default consequences may result.

2. **8-Minute Delay**:
   - Plaintiff arrived at 1:38 PM (8 minutes late).
   - Settlement conference was already scheduled to begin at 1:30 PM.
   - **No procedural order** stated that settlement conferences "close" at 1:30 PM sharp.
   - **No warning** that tardiness = default.

3. **Unique Circumstances of Family Law Case**:
   - This is a **custody matter involving a minor child (age 5)**.
   - Default judgment in custody cases is **disfavored** because the child's interests are at stake, not just the parties' contract interests.
   - Hawaii courts have stated: "[D]efault is a drastic remedy and should be sparingly used." *See* **Tobie v. Aetna Life Ins. Co.**, 82 Haw. 327 (App. 1996).

#### B. Lack of "Willful" Non-Appearance

**HFCR 55(c) standard**: "For good cause shown, the court may set aside an entry of default which has not been acted on, or may set aside a judgment of default notwithstanding the entry of default..."

**Willfulness elements:**
1. **Intentional conduct**: Plaintiff intended to attend (present at 1:38 PM).
2. **Violation of court order**: No order stated settlement conference = default proceeding.
3. **Deliberate disregard**: Plaintiff was not deliberately ignoring the proceedings.

**Conclusion**: 8-minute tardiness is **not willful default** under any standard.

#### C. Meritorious Defense / Excusable Neglect

**Excusable neglect factors** (*Tobie* test):
1. **Plaintiff's diligence**: Plaintiff appeared at 1:38 PM (not a no-show).
2. **Reasonableness of excuse**: 8-minute traffic delay is a standard, foreseeable occurrence. No extraordinary explanation needed.
3. **Promptness of relief**: Plaintiff filed motion immediately upon learning of default.
4. **Prejudice to other party**: None. Defendant would still have custody; Plaintiff merely seeks opportunity to present defenses.

**Merits of underlying case**:
- **Multiple grounds to vacate**: Lack of parenting plan, no psychological evaluation, falsified minutes, judicial misconduct, due process violations.
- **Strong factual defense**: Documentary audio evidence of physical neglect.
- **Equity of situation**: Plaintiff is pro se, not represented by counsel (entitled to more lenient default standard).

### III. LEGAL STANDARD

#### A. HFCR 55(c): Setting Aside Default

"For good cause shown, the court may set aside an entry of default...or may set aside a judgment of default...[if the] court finds that the judgment of default was entered without notice and without a hearing being held..."

**Analysis**:
- ✓ No notice that default would result
- ✓ No hearing held
- ✓ Good cause (excusable neglect + meritorious defense)

#### B. HFCR 60(b): Relief from Judgment

"On motion and upon such terms as the court deems just, the court in an action or proceeding may relieve a party...from a final judgment, order, or other decision...[for] (1) mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect..."

**Analysis**:
- ✓ Excusable neglect (8-minute delay due to traffic)
- ✓ Surprise (Plaintiff did not anticipate settlement conference = default proceeding)
- ✓ Meritorious defenses and grounds to vacate

#### C. Disfavor of Default in Family Law

Hawaii courts recognize that default should be **sparingly used** in family law matters because the interests of minor children are at stake. *See* **Amical v. Amical**, 123 Haw. 410 (2010) (family law matters warrant heightened scrutiny to protect family integrity).

### IV. PREJUDICE AND EQUITY

1. **Prejudice to Plaintiff**: Default prevents Plaintiff from presenting evidence that custody order violates HRS §571-46(b) and due process.
2. **No prejudice to Defendant**: Defendant retains custody; Plaintiff merely seeks opportunity to be heard.
3. **Equity**: Plaintiff is pro se and entitled to generous default standards; child's interests warrant careful adjudication.
4. **Judicial integrity**: Setting aside default serves justice and accuracy of proceedings.

### V. RELIEF REQUESTED

Plaintiff requests this Court:

1. **Set aside** the default judgment entered April 2, 2025.
2. **Vacate** any orders entered as a result of the default judgment.
3. **Restore** Plaintiff's right to participate in all settlement conferences and hearings.
4. **Direct** that settlement conferences shall not result in default without **prior written notice** complying with HFCR 55(b)(2).
5. **Grant** Plaintiff full opportunity to present evidence and be heard on all pending issues.
6. **Such other and further relief** as the Court deems just and equitable.
