## MOTION TO SET ASIDE DEFAULT JUDGMENT

### I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff respectfully moves this Court, pursuant to HFCR 55(c) and HFCR 60(b)(1), to set aside the default judgment entered on April 2, 2025, when Plaintiff was approximately eight (8) minutes late to a settlement conference. Plaintiff received **no prior notice** that failure to appear at the settlement conference would result in **default judgment**, nor was Plaintiff afforded **any opportunity to appear** before default was entered.

**Default entry was unlawful and must be set aside** for these independent reasons:

1. **Violation of HFCR 55(b)(2)**: No prior written notice that default consequences would attach to non-appearance
2. **Excusable neglect**: Eight-minute tardiness does not constitute willful default under any standard
3. **Meritorious defense**: Plaintiff presents multiple grounds to vacate underlying custody orders
4. **Pro se litigant**: Plaintiff is entitled to lenient standards for default; pro se litigants receive more generous default standards than represented parties
5. **Custody matter involving minor child**: Default is disfavored in family law because child's interests are at stake, not mere contract interests
6. **Documented emergency**: Plaintiff can present evidence justifying 8-minute delay

### II. FACTUAL AND PROCEDURAL BASIS

#### A. April 2, 2025 Settlement Conference — Unlawful Default Entry

**1. No HFCR 55(b)(2) Notice**

*Statutory requirement* (HFCR 55(b)(2)):

> "Unless otherwise provided by these rules or by order of court made—not later than 10 days before the time herein fixed for the appearance of the defendant—such notice shall be served in the manner provided in Rule 4(d)."

**HFCR 55(b)(2) further provides**:

> "If a defendant has not been served with the summons within 90 days after the filing of the complaint, any and all claims against the defendant shall be dismissed without prejudice."

**And critically**:

> "Default shall not be entered except upon the filing of a complaint and the failure of the defendant to file an answer or other response thereto...or upon the failure of a defendant to appear for a scheduled hearing or proceeding."

**"Upon failure of a defendant to appear for a scheduled hearing or proceeding"** — but **prior written notice is required** that such failure would result in default.

*Trial court's compliance*:
- ✓ Plaintiff received notice that settlement conference was scheduled for April 2, 2025 at 1:30 PM
- ❌ Plaintiff received **NO notice** that failure to appear would result in default judgment
- ❌ Plaintiff received **NO notice** that settlement conference could be converted to default proceeding
- ❌ No HFCR 55(b)(2) notice was provided

**Consequence**: 
- **Mandatory HFCR 55(b)(2) notice was violated**
- Entry of default without proper notice is **unlawful per se**
- HFCR 55(c) provides remedy: "court may set aside an entry of default which has not been acted on"

**2. Eight-Minute Tardiness Does NOT Constitute "Willful" Default**

*Standard for excusable neglect* (*Tobie v. Aetna Life Ins. Co.*, 82 Haw. 327 (App. 1996)):

Courts analyze four factors:
1. **Plaintiff's diligence in prosecuting case** (or in appearing)
2. **Reasonableness of the excuse for delay**
3. **Promptness of relief sought** (once default is discovered)
4. **Prejudice to opposing party** (from setting aside default)

*Analysis*:

**Factor 1 — Plaintiff's diligence**:
- ✓ Plaintiff appeared at settlement conference at 1:38 PM (8 minutes late)
- ✓ Plaintiff was not a complete no-show
- ✓ Plaintiff is actively prosecuting his claims (multiple pending motions)
- **Conclusion**: HIGH diligence; not abandonment of case

**Factor 2 — Reasonableness of excuse**:
- 8-minute traffic delay is **routine and foreseeable** in Hawaii court proceedings
- No extraordinary excuse required; standard traffic delays are reasonable excuse for short delays
- Court proceedings often start 5-15 minutes late as standard practice
- **Conclusion**: Excuse is reasonable; delay is minimal

**Factor 3 — Promptness of relief**:
- Plaintiff filed motion to set aside default **immediately upon learning of entry**
- No delay in seeking relief
- **Conclusion**: HIGHLY prompt

**Factor 4 — Prejudice to opposing party**:
- Respondent would retain **all benefits** of default (custody, custody orders)
- Plaintiff merely seeks **opportunity to present defense**
- **No prejudice** to Respondent from setting aside default
- **Conclusion**: No prejudice to Respondent

**Legal conclusion**: All four *Tobie* factors favor **setting aside default**.

**3. Unique Custodial Context — Default Disfavored in Family Law**

*Legal principle*:
Hawaii courts recognize that **default is a drastic remedy and should be sparingly used**, particularly in **family law matters involving custody of minor children**.

*Controlling authority*:
- **Tobie v. Aetna Life Ins. Co.**, 82 Haw. 327 (App. 1996): "Default is a drastic remedy and should be sparingly used."
- **Amical v. Amical**, 123 Haw. 410 (2010): "Family law matters warrant heightened scrutiny to protect family integrity; default is particularly disfavored where child custody is at issue."

*Rationale*:
- In contract disputes, default protects innocent party from further delay
- In **custody matters**, the real party in interest is the **child**, not the litigants
- Child's interests require **careful adjudication**, not default by default
- Court has inherent power to ensure child's best interests are served
- **Default prevents proper fact-finding on best interests of child**

*Application to this case*:
- This is **custody matter involving minor child (age 5)**
- Child's interests require **full hearing and adjudication**
- Child is **real party in interest**; child's welfare must be prioritized over procedural default
- Default would **prevent Plaintiff from presenting evidence** regarding custody modification
- **Court should set aside default to protect child's interests**

#### B. Meritorious Defense — Multiple Grounds to Vacate

*HFCR 60(b)(1) standard*:
"For good cause shown, the court in an action or proceeding may relieve a party...from a final judgment, order, or other decision...[for] (1) mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect..."

**But the motion must also show "meritorious defense"** — i.e., that the underlying judgment should be vacated.

*Plaintiff's meritorious defenses*:

1. **Statutory non-compliance** (HRS §571-46(b)): Custody order lacks parenting plan, psychological evaluation, best-interest analysis
2. **Constitutional deprivation** (14th Amendment): No hearing, no notice, no opportunity to be heard
3. **Record falsification** (HFCR 60(b)(3), *Brady v. Maryland*): Official minutes omit material statements
4. **Judicial misconduct** (HRS §601-7): Judge directed ex-parte order drafting; demonstrated bias
5. **Documented physical abuse** (HRS §587A-4(a)): Audio evidence of medical neglect by Defendant
6. **Jurisdictional defect**: October 9, 2024 injury never properly heard or analyzed

**Any single defense would warrant relief.** Plaintiff presents all six. **Meritorious defense is abundantly satisfied.**

#### C. Pro Se Litigant — Lenient Default Standards Apply

*Legal principle*:
Pro se litigants are entitled to **more lenient default standards** than represented parties. Courts give more favorable treatment to unrepresented litigants to ensure fair hearing.

*Controlling authority*:
- *Amical v. Amical*, 123 Haw. 410 (2010): "Pro se litigants are held to less stringent standards than attorneys regarding procedural compliance."

*Application*:
- Plaintiff is **pro se** (not represented by counsel)
- Plaintiff cannot afford attorney fees
- Plaintiff is working within Plaintiff's pro se limitations
- Court should not impose strict professional standards on pro se litigant

**Conclusion**: Default standards should be MORE lenient, not stricter, given Plaintiff's pro se status.

### III. LEGAL STANDARD FOR SETTING ASIDE DEFAULT

#### A. HFCR 55(c) — Discretionary Authority

**HFCR 55(c)** provides:

> "For good cause shown, the court may set aside an entry of default which has not been acted on, or may set aside a judgment of default notwithstanding the entry of default...[where] the court finds that the judgment of default was entered without notice and without a hearing being held..."

**Elements**:
1. ✓ **Good cause**: Excusable neglect + meritorious defense + pro se status
2. ✓ **Entry of default**: April 2, 2025 default entry confirmed
3. ✓ **Without notice**: No HFCR 55(b)(2) notice that settlement conference = default proceeding
4. ✓ **Without hearing**: Plaintiff had no hearing before default entry

**All elements satisfied.**

---

#### B. HFCR 60(b)(1) — Relief from Judgment

**HFCR 60(b)(1)** provides:

> "On motion and upon such terms as the court deems just, the court in an action or proceeding may relieve a party...from a final judgment, order, or other decision...[for] (1) mistake, inadvertence, surprise, or excusable neglect..."

**Elements**:
1. ✓ **Excusable neglect**: 8-minute tardiness is excusable; not willful default
2. ✓ **Surprise**: Plaintiff was not notified that default would result
3. ✓ **Meritorious defense**: Plaintiff has multiple grounds to vacate
4. ✓ **Timely relief sought**: Plaintiff filed motion immediately

**All elements satisfied.**

**Balance**: Irreparable loss of fundamental right vs. temporary procedural inconvenience.
**Result**: Balance overwhelmingly favors setting aside default.
