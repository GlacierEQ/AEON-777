### JURISDICTIONAL BASIS

42 U.S.C. § 1983 provides: "Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage, of any State or Territory, subjects any citizen of the United States or any other person within the jurisdiction thereof to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured."

### CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHTS IMPLICATED

1. **Due Process Clause, 14th Amendment**: Deprivation of liberty interest in parental rights and child custody without due process
2. **Equal Protection Clause, 14th Amendment**: Selective and discriminatory enforcement against pro se litigant
3. **Fundamental Liberty Interest**: Parental right to care, custody, and control of one's children

### CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

#### Parental Rights as Fundamental Liberty Interest

*Troxel v. Granville*, 530 U.S. 57 (2000): "The liberty interest at issue in this case—the interest of parents in the care, custody, and control of their children—is perhaps the oldest of the fundamental liberty interests recognized by this Court."

Deprivation of this right without due process violates the Fourteenth Amendment and is actionable under § 1983.

#### Due Process Requirements

*Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976) requires:
1. **Notice**: Actual notice of proceedings and decisions
2. **Opportunity to be Heard**: Fair opportunity to present one's case
3. **Impartial Tribunal**: Decision-making by an impartial judge or officer

Defendants violated each of these requirements.
