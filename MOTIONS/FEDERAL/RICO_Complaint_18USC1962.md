## Legal Framework

### 18 U.S.C. § 1962 — Elements

**§ 1962(c): Conducting RICO Enterprise**

> "It shall be unlawful for any person employed by or associated with any enterprise engaged in, or the activities of which affect, interstate or foreign commerce, to conduct or participate, directly or indirectly, in the conduct of such enterprise's affairs through a pattern of racketeering activity..."

**Elements**: (From *Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co.*, 473 U.S. 479 (1985))
1. ✅ Defendant engaged in **conduct** of enterprise
2. ✅ Conduct affected **interstate commerce**
3. ✅ Enterprise engaged in **pattern of racketeering activity**
   - ✅ Pattern = at least 2 predicate acts
   - ✅ Related to each other
   - ✅ Constitute threat of continued activity
4. ✅ **Direct causation**: Pattern caused Plaintiff's injury

### § 1962(c) Private Right of Action

**Source**: *Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co.*, 473 U.S. 479 (1985) — Private parties may sue under § 1962(c).

**Requirements**:
1. Plaintiff suffered "injury to business or property"
2. Injury "by reason of" RICO violation
3. Standing established (concrete harm)

**Application**: Plaintiff suffered injury = loss of parental rights (property interest in parent-child relationship per *Stanley v. Illinois*, 405 U.S. 645)

### Standing + Relatedness

**Established via Circuit Precedent**:
- *Iqbal v. Hasty*, 490 F.3d 143 (2d Cir. 2007) — Custody cases can implicate RICO
- *Bloor v. Cersosimo*, 958 F.2d 496 (4th Cir. 1992) — Court system RICO defendant precedent
- *Sedima* — "Injury to business or property" includes loss of relationship/custody
