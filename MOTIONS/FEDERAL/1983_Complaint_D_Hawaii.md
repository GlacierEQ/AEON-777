## Legal Standards

### A. § 1983 Liability

Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983:

> "Every person who, under color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, custom, or usage of any State... subjects, or causes to be subjected, any citizen of the United States... to the deprivation of any rights, privileges, or immunities secured by the Constitution and laws, shall be liable to the party injured..."

**Elements**:
1. ✅ Defendant acted under color of state law
2. ✅ Constitutional or statutory right was violated
3. ✅ Violation was committed by Defendant
4. ✅ Causation and damages

### B. Qualified Immunity Defense (Overcome)

**The right to due process in custody matters is clearly established:**

- *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976) — Due process requires notice and opportunity to be heard
- *Leis v. Flynt*, 439 U.S. 438 (1979) — State cannot deprive person of fundamental liberties without due process
- *Santosky v. Kramer*, 455 U.S. 745 (1982) — Clear and convincing evidence required in custody/termination cases
- *Stanley v. Illinois*, 405 U.S. 645 (1972) — Parental rights are fundamental; cannot be deprived without hearing

**Defendants cannot claim qualified immunity where:**
1. Constitutional violation is beyond question
2. Reasonable official would have known conduct violated law
3. Pattern of violations (21/21 adverse rulings = clear pattern)
