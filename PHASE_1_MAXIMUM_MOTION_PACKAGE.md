#### IV. LEGAL ARGUMENTS

**A. Due Process Violation - 42 USC §1983**

The Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment requires that judicial proceedings be fair and that the petitioner have a meaningful opportunity to be heard. When a judge files evidence on behalf of one party and seals it from the other, the judge becomes advocate rather than neutral arbiter.

*Ex parte testimony from judge = disqualification*

**B. Bias Requiring Disqualification - 28 USC §144**

Any justice or judge of the United States shall disqualify himself in any proceeding in which his impartiality might reasonably be questioned. Here, the judge's actions raise not just questions, but demonstrate actual bias:

- Filing exhibits for respondent (advocate role)
- Sealing exhibits from petitioner (partisan action)
- 235 exhibits with zero petitioner participation (structural bias)

**C. Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 42.1 Violation**

FRAP 42.1 requires appeals courts to disqualify judges when impartiality is questioned. This case was never appealed—yet the bias is apparent in the initial proceedings.

**D. Conspiracy to Deprive - 42 USC §1985**

When a judge acts in concert with a respondent to deprive a petitioner of constitutional rights, that is conspiracy under federal law. The pattern of judge-filed exhibits on respondent's behalf suggests collaboration.
