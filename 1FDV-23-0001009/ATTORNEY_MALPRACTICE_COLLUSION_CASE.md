## 1. Executive theory

The evidence supports a serious claim that counsel accepted and performed representation, knew a contested hearing and substantive strategy issues were pending, then expressly declined to read a client email seeking hearing strategy, redirected the client to another person, limited her role to withdrawal, and left the client to proceed pro se during the transition corridor. The July 22, 2024 email is direct evidence of the conduct; it is not merely a client allegation.

The related **coordinated-conduct/collusion theory** is narrower and must be proved separately. It asks whether Yamatani, Greg Ryan, the firm, opposing counsel, or other actors coordinated communications, withdrawal, file turnover, lien activity, docket presentation, or litigation positioning in a way that foreseeably prejudiced Casey. Current evidence supports investigation and pleading of a possible coordinated sequence; it does not yet establish an agreement or shared unlawful purpose.

The docket-integrity theory is separate again: whether counsel's name and service/filing identifiers disappeared from later docket-face/header displays after withdrawal, and whether the change was an ordinary court-system update, an authorized appearance change, or an unexplained alteration. It requires page-level comparison of original docket PDFs/NEFs and later docket-face records.

## 3. Malpractice / abandonment theory

### Duty and relationship

- Retainer, fee records, engagement scope, billing ledger, appearance records, and Yamatani's substantive filings establish the attorney-client relationship and scope.
- The May 16 substantive motion and June hearing schedule show active litigation duties immediately before withdrawal.

### Breach indicators

- Explicit refusal to read a client email seeking strategy for an upcoming hearing.
- Redirection of the client to Greg while Yamatani was still handling the withdrawal corridor.
- Limitation of role to withdrawal without a verified, adequate transition plan.
- PACT documents reportedly retrieved but not read before counsel's exit.
- File turnover not confirmed until September, after the July transition and August hearings.

### Causation / prejudice to prove

Build a hearing-specific prejudice record, not a general narrative:

- What exactly did Casey ask in the email?
- What hearing date, deadlines, exhibits, or strategic choices were then pending?
- What advice, preparation, or filing was not provided?
- What did Casey do pro se instead, and when?
- What ruling, loss of position, missed opportunity, cost, or child/family impact followed?
- Which damages are supported by records rather than estimates?

### Damages categories to document

- Fees paid for work not performed or not reasonably completed.
- Costs of emergency pro se response, reconstruction, or replacement counsel.
- Hearing-specific prejudice and adverse procedural consequences.
- Consequential financial and family impacts only where tied to records and a defensible causal chain.

## 4. Coordinated-conduct / collusion theory

### Working hypothesis

The July withdrawal, refusal to read/advise, redirection to Greg, delayed file transfer, lien explanation, and any later docket-face change may form a coordinated sequence that protected institutional or opposing interests while shifting the burden and risk to Casey. This is a theory to test—not a fact to state conclusively.

### Actors and evidence targets

| Actor / group | Current evidence | What must be proved | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micky Yamatani | July 22 direct email; withdrawal filings; substantive filings | Knowledge of hearing need, decision to disengage, scope of transition, billing | Strong conduct evidence; intent pending |
| Greg Ryan | July 22 redirection; forwarded materials; September chain | What Greg received, advised, did, or failed to do; role and authority | Direct communication lead; full thread review needed |
| Firm / billing personnel | August 2 complaint; lien response; Clio chain | Who authorized lien, when, why, and what records support the explanation | Documentary lead; intent not established |
| Opposing counsel / other participants | Docket and communications | Any coordination, notice, or advantage tied to withdrawal/file/lien events | Investigation only |
| Court docket system / clerk records | Initial attorney attribution; later header observation | Whether display changed through ordinary update or unexplained alteration; audit trail | Page-level comparison pending |

### Evidence that would materially strengthen collusion proof

- Emails or texts showing advance coordination about withdrawal, the lien, file access, or Casey's inability to respond.
- Calendar/meeting metadata linking actors during the critical corridor.
- Billing entries inconsistent with the claimed work or showing strategic timing.
- Clio audit logs showing upload, deletion, export, access, or delayed sharing.
- Certified docket/JEFS audit history showing who changed attorney/service information and when.
- Communications demonstrating that a party knew Casey was unrepresented yet used the transition to obtain a procedural advantage.
